Wine Guide

Wine knowledge for wherever you are in your journey.

🍷 How to Taste Wine

Start by looking at the wine. Then smell it before swirling. Swirl gently and smell again. Take a small sip and pay attention to sweetness, acidity, tannin, alcohol, body and flavour.

👁️ Appearance Guide

White: Pale Lemon → Lemon → Gold → Amber

Red: Purple → Ruby → Garnet → Tawny

Rosé: Pale Pink → Salmon → Orange

💧 Understanding Acidity

Acidity creates freshness and makes your mouth water. Pay attention to salivation after swallowing or spitting. More mouth-watering sensation usually suggests higher acidity.

👃 How to Smell Wine

Smell first without swirling. Then swirl gently and smell again. Start broad: fruit, floral, herbal, spice, oak and earth. Only then become specific.

🍂 What is Tannin?

Tannin creates a drying or gripping sensation on your gums, tongue and cheeks. It is particularly noticeable in many red wines.

⚖️ Understanding Body

Body describes the overall weight and texture of a wine. Think of the difference between skim milk, whole milk and cream.

🌡️ Serving Wine

Temperature changes how wine tastes and smells. Whites are generally served cooler than reds, but serving wine extremely cold can hide aromas and flavour.

🍽️ Food Pairing

Acidic food generally needs an equally acidic wine. Sweet wine should usually be at least as sweet as the dessert. Salt can soften the perception of tannin. High alcohol can intensify spicy heat.

🏷️ Reading a Wine Label

Depending on where the wine comes from, the label may emphasize the grape variety, the region, the producer or a combination of these. Vintage tells you the year in which most of the grapes were harvested.

🎯 Taste for Yourself

Taste first and record your observations. Research the wine afterward. Your own perception is the most important part of your journal.
